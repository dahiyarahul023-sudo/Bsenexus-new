## Intro

If your investments are spread across a Zerodha demat, a Groww mutual fund folio, an EPF account and maybe some US stocks, no single broker app shows you everything at once. That's what a portfolio tracker is for: one dashboard for everything you own, with returns math done for you. This guide compares the seven worth knowing in India in 2026, explains what they can and cannot do, and ends with the honest gap every tracker shares — plus how to cover it.

## H2: What to look for in a portfolio tracker

Six things separate a good tracker from a glorified spreadsheet:

- **Auto-sync vs manual entry.** The best trackers pull holdings automatically from your brokers, MF folios, EPF and bank accounts. Manual-entry trackers are cheaper but go stale fast.
- **Returns analytics.** XIRR (your true annualised return across irregular investments) is the number that matters; check whether the tool computes it across all accounts, not just per-fund.
- **Goal mapping.** Can you tag investments to goals (retirement, house, child's education) and see each goal's progress?
- **Coverage.** Stocks, mutual funds, EPF, PPF, FDs, NPS, US equities — how many asset classes does it actually track?
- **Cost.** Most core tracking is free; paid tiers usually add advisory, tax tools or deeper analytics.
- **Data privacy.** Auto-sync means handing over account access. Understand what you grant, and check the app's regulatory standing before you connect everything.

### H3: INDmoney — best for all-in-one net-worth aggregation

The closest thing India has to a single financial dashboard. INDmoney pulls data from bank accounts, demat accounts, MF folios, EPF, NPS and credit cards into one consolidated net-worth view, and also supports Indian and US stock investing under RBI's LRS allowance plus zero-commission direct mutual funds. The company claims 10 million+ registered users. Its advisory layer is algorithmic rather than fiduciary — the "wealth managers" on paid plans aren't SEBI-registered advisors — so treat it as a superb tracker first and an advisor second. It even pushes stock news with sentiment tags, handy but still news, not the raw filing.

### H3: Kuvera — best for goal-based MF tracking

Kuvera's strength is structure: create goals and assign individual stocks and funds to each, so you always know which investment serves which goal. Unusually for a tracker, it lets you buy and sell shares directly through a linked Zerodha Kite account (each transaction still needs your CDSL approval, so holdings stay safe). It also tracks mutual funds, EPF and fixed deposits. If your main frustration is "I have goals but my investments aren't mapped to them," this is the one.

### H3: ET Money — best for data-driven insights + tax planning

ET Money's identity is intelligence: Fund Report Cards, Portfolio Health Checks, multi-asset allocation strategies and tax-planning tools, all wrapped around direct mutual fund investing. One honest caveat from December 2025 reporting: ET Money has moved new investors to a subscription model (ET Money Genius) with regular funds, stepping back from the free-direct-funds positioning it was once known for — so read the current pricing carefully before you sign up. Acquired by 360 One WAM in June 2024.

### H3: Groww — best if you already invest on Groww

If your money already lives on Groww, its native holdings view is the simplest tracker you'll find: direct mutual funds, stocks, IPOs, FDs, digital gold and US stocks in one clean, beginner-friendly app. Groww is India's most-downloaded investment platform and the largest MF platform by registered users (12 crore+), and it recently received an AMC license. The limitation is the obvious one: it's a tracker for Groww holdings, not an aggregator across brokers.

### H3: Zerodha Console — best for Zerodha users

Console is Zerodha's answer to portfolio tracking: your Kite holdings plus Coin mutual funds in one view, with the tax P&L reports that make filing season dramatically easier. Zerodha remains India's largest stockbroker by active clients, and Console comes free with the account. Like Groww's view, it's bounded by the ecosystem — excellent inside Zerodha, not a cross-broker dashboard.

### H3: Tickertape — best for holdings + research in one view

Tickertape merges tracking with analysis: connect your broker (Zerodha, Upstox, Angel One and others) and see holdings alongside 200+ filters, scorecards, forecasts and peer comparisons. One real limitation to know: it connects to only one broker account at a time, so multi-broker investors can't see everything together. Free basic tier; premium at ₹249/month, ₹699 for 3 months, or ₹2,399/year.

### H3: Yahoo Finance — best free basic tracker

The old reliable. Yahoo Finance's free portfolio tools combine tracking with market news, earnings calendars, analyst ratings and watchlists — no account aggregation, just clean, dependable basics that millions of investors still use. If you want a no-cost, no-commitment way to watch holdings and read the news around them, it remains one of the best free options available.

### H3: BSE Nexus — best for tracking news on your holdings

To be clear: **BSE Nexus is not a portfolio tracker**, and we don't rank it as one. It has no brokerage sync and no XIRR — the trackers above do that job. It belongs on this list because it covers the one thing every tracker above structurally misses — **what's happening to what you own, right now**.

Holdings trackers show your portfolio as it was at the last sync. But results, board meetings, block deals, auditor resignations and clarifications hit the exchange as filings *first*, and prices move on them within minutes. Some trackers push repackaged news (INDmoney tags news sentiment; Yahoo bundles market news) — useful, but still news, not the source. BSE Nexus watches the exchange itself: every BSE filing as it lands, a board-meeting and results calendar, and instant alerts on your watchlist companies.

The filings feed and results calendar are free; Pro (from ₹59/week) adds unlimited watchlists, instant alerts on every filing for your watched stocks, and AI filing summaries — with a 1-week free Pro trial on sign-in, no card required.

## H2: Free vs paid — what you actually get

Honest truth: core tracking is free almost everywhere on this list. INDmoney, Kuvera, Groww, Zerodha Console, Tickertape's basic tier and Yahoo Finance all give you a working dashboard for nothing. Paid plans mostly buy three things: human or algorithmic advisory (INDmoney's wealth manager, Kuvera's tiers), deeper analytics and forecasts (Tickertape premium), or — in ET Money's new model — the platform experience itself via subscription. Start free; pay only when a specific paid feature earns its keep. And always check the current price on the vendor's own site — as ET Money's 2025 pivot showed, pricing models change.

## H2: Manual vs auto-sync: the real tradeoff

Auto-sync is the magic — holdings that update themselves across brokers, MFs and EPF. The price is access: the tracker connects to your accounts via your email, PAN and account credentials or aggregator frameworks. Manual-entry trackers ask for nothing but your time, and your time is exactly why they go stale. There's no wrong answer, only a comfort level: if you auto-sync, use strong unique passwords, enable two-factor authentication everywhere, and periodically review which apps hold access to your accounts.

## H2: What's new in portfolio tracking in 2026

Three shifts are reshaping trackers this year. First, **AI is doing the analysis**: platforms now study your allocation, measure portfolio risk and suggest rebalancing instead of just showing numbers. Second, **unified asset tracking** is becoming the default expectation — stocks, ETFs, crypto, cash and alternatives in a single dashboard rather than separate accounts. Third, **tax automation** is maturing: capital-gains reports, tax summaries and tax-loss information generated in-app, cutting manual work at filing time. Pick a tracker that's moving with these, not one frozen in 2022.

## H2: The missing layer: news on your portfolio

Your tracker shows **what you own**. Nothing on this list — not even the best of them — shows you **what's happening to what you own** in real time. A results announcement at 4:30 PM moves the price at 9:15 AM the next morning; a tracker shows you the damage after the fact. News bundles help, but they're repackaged and delayed relative to the exchange.

That's the gap BSE Nexus fills: watchlist-based instant alerts on every BSE filing for your stocks — results, board meetings, block deals, clarifications — the moment they hit the exchange. Use any tracker above to *organise* the portfolio; use a filings feed to *protect* it from news you never saw coming.

## H2: Frequently asked questions

**Q1. Which is the best portfolio tracker app in India?**
There isn't one winner — it depends on your need. INDmoney leads for all-in-one net-worth aggregation, Kuvera for goal-based tracking, ET Money for data-driven insights, Tickertape for holdings plus research, and Yahoo Finance for a free basic option. Most core tracking is free, so try two before committing.

**Q2. Is INDmoney safe? How does it access my accounts?**
INDmoney aggregates your accounts by connecting to them — via linked email, PAN and account connections across banks, demat, MF folios, EPF and NPS. That convenience means granting account access, so use strong unique passwords and two-factor authentication. Note its advisory layer is algorithmic, not fiduciary: the "wealth managers" are relationship managers backed by algorithms, not SEBI-registered investment advisers.

**Q3. Can I track stocks across Zerodha, Groww and ICICI in one app?**
Yes — that's exactly what aggregators like INDmoney are built for: one dashboard across brokers. Note the limits, though: Tickertape connects to only one broker at a time, and broker-native views (Groww, Zerodha Console) only show their own ecosystem. For true cross-broker tracking, use a dedicated aggregator.

**Q4. Is there a free portfolio tracker in India?**
Yes, several. INDmoney, Kuvera, Groww, Zerodha Console and Tickertape all offer free core tracking; Yahoo Finance is entirely free. Paid tiers add advisory, forecasts or deeper analytics — the tracking itself costs nothing on most of them.

**Q5. Kuvera vs INDmoney — which is better?**
For pure aggregation across the most asset classes, INDmoney wins. For goal-based structure — mapping investments to goals — plus the ability to buy/sell via a linked Zerodha Kite account, Kuvera wins. Many investors use INDmoney for the dashboard and Kuvera-style goal discipline inside whichever app they prefer.

**Q6. How do I track XIRR across all my investments?**
XIRR is your true annualised return across irregular investments and withdrawals — the one number that lets you compare a 3-year SIP against a lump sum fairly. Aggregators like INDmoney and broker tools like Zerodha Console compute returns across your accounts; per-fund XIRR is available in most MF apps. For XIRR across *everything*, you need your holdings aggregated in one place first.

**Q7. Is there an app that alerts me when my stocks announce results?**
Most trackers don't do this — they show holdings, not live events. Some push repackaged stock news. For the actual event as it happens, you need the exchange feed itself: BSE Nexus sends instant alerts on every BSE filing — results, board meetings, block deals — for companies on your watchlist.
